Why Availability Zones is Correct

Target: < 2ms Latency

Availability Zones (AZs) are physically distinct datacenter locations within an individual Azure region. Each zone possesses independent cooling, power, and networking infrastructure. Because zones inside the same region are interconnected through an ultra-fast, high-capacity private optical network, workloads benefit from resilient fault boundaries while maintaining round-trip network latencies typically well under 2 milliseconds.

verified AZ-900 Exam Key: "Same region" + "datacenter failure protection" + "minimal latency (<2ms)" = Availability Zones.

Why the Tempting Alternative (Cross-Region) is NOT the Best Answer

Target: ≥ 300 Miles

While Azure Region Pairs deliver robust disaster recovery against wide-scale regional catastrophes (such as natural events or broad grid shutdowns), paired regions are intentionally located hundreds of miles apart (at least 300 miles within the same geography). Replicating data across such vast geographic distances unavoidably increases latency beyond synchronous transactional web requirements, conflicting with Contoso's strict sub-2ms constraint.

info Remember the Difference: Cross-Region Replication solves regional disasters (≥300 miles) but cannot guarantee sub-2ms latency.

Azure Region

A geographical perimeter containing at least one, but potentially multiple datacenters connected via dedicated regional network.

public 60+ regions globally
Availability Zone

Unique physical locations within a single region. Minimum of three separate zones exist per AZ-enabled region with independent utilities.

speed <2ms fiber latency
Region Pair

Two distinct regions in the same geography located at least 300 miles apart to ensure continuous business continuity during outages.

distance ≥300 miles isolation
